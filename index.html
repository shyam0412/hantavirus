INDIA STATUS

No Cases
🇮🇳

No confirmed Hantavirus cases in India as of May 11, 2026

Two Indian crew members who were aboard the MV Hondius cruise ship have been confirmed asymptomatic and are under observation per international health protocols. ICMR's National Institute of Virology states the cases appear isolated with no immediate public health threat to India. NCDC, IDSP and IHR-NFP India are actively monitoring the situation in coordination with WHO.

India has adequate diagnostic capacity through the nationwide Viral Research and Diagnostic Laboratory (VRDL) network. Senior officials from IDSP and IHR-NFP India attended a high-level review conference convened by the Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC) under IDSP and NCDC to evaluate preparedness measures. Experts from ICMR and AIIMS have stated hantavirus does not pose an immediate public health threat to India, as the virus does not spread easily between humans, and the rodent species carrying the Andes strain are not commonly found in Indian households.
While asymptomatic exposure to hantaviruses has been documented in India, serious outbreaks have not been reported. Dr. Surendran R., Consultant in Infectious Disease at SIMS Hospital Chennai, notes: "Hantavirus is mainly transmitted through contact with wild and non-domesticated rodents — not generally found in human dwellings. So people in Indian cities are not at risk." However, climate change and poor garbage disposal are increasing rodent populations. From India's perspective, leptospirosis remains a far bigger rodent-borne threat currently. That said, awareness and hygiene remain important.
If you were aboard a ship in the Atlantic region between April 6–28, 2026 or had close contact with someone who was — monitor yourself for symptoms for 45 days. The incubation period for Andes hantavirus can be several weeks. If you develop fever, severe muscle aches, or breathing difficulty, contact your nearest government hospital or call the NCDC helpline immediately. WHO advises frequent handwashing, symptom vigilance, and self-isolation if you develop symptoms.

KNOW THE SYMPTOMS

● Stage 1 — Days 1–5 · Early (Flu-like)
  • High fever and chills
  • Severe muscle aches (especially thighs, hips, back)
  • Intense headache
  • Fatigue and dizziness
  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  • Abdominal pain

⚠ Easy to mistake for flu, dengue or viral fever. Don't ignore if you have rodent or travel exposure.

● Stage 2 — Days 5–10 · Critical (Seek Emergency Care)
  • Shortness of breath — URGENT SIGN
  • Cough with fluid / postnasal drainage
  • Fluid buildup in lungs
  • Low blood pressure / shock
  • Reduced heart efficiency
  • Organ failure in severe cases

🚨 Stage 2 escalates within hours. Call 108 or go to nearest ICU-capable hospital immediately.

Important: There is currently no approved vaccine or antiviral cure for hantavirus. Early supportive care in an ICU dramatically improves survival chances. The faster you get to hospital, the better.

PREVENTION

Best Defence

No vaccine exists. Prevention is your only shield. These are simple, effective measures endorsed by WHO and CDC.

🐭

Avoid Rodent Contact

Don't touch mice, rats, their nests, droppings, or urine — especially in warehouses, storage areas, ships, and rural areas. This is the primary route of infection.

🧹

Safe Rodent Cleanup

Never sweep or vacuum rodent droppings dry — this aerosolizes the virus. Wet the area with bleach solution first, then clean with gloves and a mask. Dispose in sealed bags.

🪟

Ventilate Before Entering

If entering a closed space (shed, cabin, storeroom) that may have had rodents — open doors and windows 30 minutes before entering. Don't let dust settle on you.

🧤

Wear Gloves & Mask

When working in rodent-prone areas, wear rubber gloves and an N95 mask. The virus spreads through airborne particles from infected rodent secretions.

🍱

Seal Food Storage

Keep food in airtight containers. Don't leave grain, rice, or food scraps accessible. Secure garbage bins. Reducing food access reduces rodent population near you.

🧼

Handwash Frequently

Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water after being outdoors, in agricultural areas, or near any animal exposure. The WHO recommends this especially for travellers now.

🚢

Travel Precautions

If you were on a cruise ship or in South America recently — monitor for symptoms for 45 days. Inform your doctor of travel history if you develop fever or breathing problems.

⚡

Act Fast on Symptoms

Early ICU care dramatically improves survival. Don't wait for Stage 2. If you have fever + muscle aches + rodent or travel exposure — see a doctor the same day. Don't delay.
